# Landing Page and Conversion Playbook

Use for marketing pages, product launches, feature pages, waitlists, service pages, and conversion-focused sections.

## 1. Define the conversion

Write a one-line brief: audience + offer + desired outcome + primary action. Identify what counts as a completed conversion and what happens next. If information is missing, use explicit assumptions; do not invent proof.

## 2. Understand visitor context

Capture the visitor's likely awareness, traffic source, current problem, desired outcome, and top objections. Align the headline and first-screen visual with that context. Do not promise more than the product delivers.

## 3. Select a page narrative

Choose a structure that fits the offer:
- Product-led: hero, product demonstration, benefits, workflow, proof, FAQ, CTA.
- Education-led: problem, explanation, approach, examples, proof, objections, CTA.
- High-intent: offer, key value, proof, terms, short FAQ, CTA.
- Comparison: decision criteria, honest comparison, use cases, evidence, CTA.

Use sections selectively. Do not force a fixed section count or a mandatory decorative tagline animation.

## 4. Hero acceptance criteria

Within the first viewport, visitors should understand:
- What the product/service is.
- Who it is for.
- What useful outcome it provides.
- What to do next.

Use one dominant CTA. A secondary action may exist if it supports a different but clearly subordinate intent. Pair claims with evidence or qualify them.

## 5. Copy framework

- Headline: concrete outcome or category for a defined audience.
- Supporting copy: what it does and how it helps.
- CTA: action plus expected result (for example, “Book a demo”).
- Benefits: outcome first, feature detail second.
- Proof: only verified customer stories, metrics, logos, or quotations.
- Objections: answer real concerns about setup, compatibility, cost, security, support, and cancellation where relevant.

Avoid generic hype, fabricated urgency, fake social proof, and unsubstantiated superlatives. Use product-specific language and believable examples.

## 6. Visual narrative

Select a visual direction that reinforces the promise: product UI, editorial typography, illustration, photography, original 3D, diagram, or a combination. Maintain a consistent art direction across sections. Use real product captures when available; otherwise label illustrative concepts honestly.

## 7. SEO and accessibility

Use a descriptive title, meta description, semantic heading hierarchy, meaningful link text, alt text, keyboard access, focus states, and tested contrast. Add structured data only when it accurately represents visible content. Keep campaign pages out of search when the campaign is temporary or intentionally private.

## 8. Build and test

Build section by section, starting with hero and core narrative. Verify links, forms, validation, success/error feedback, mobile layout, content wrapping, page performance, and metadata. Do not claim a conversion works unless the real submission path is connected and tested.
